02 — How a unit moves through the shop

Under one roof,
end to end.

Because test capability is in house, a unit never leaves our building between disassembly and release. No subcontractor queue inside your turn time.

01

Intake

Part number, condition and timing logged. If the number isn't in our published capabilities, send it anyway — we don't publish everything we support.

02

Disassembly

Full teardown by technicians who specialize in that component family — not generalists rotating through unfamiliar hardware.

03

Cleaning & inspection

Abrasive blast area, cleaning tanks and detailed inspection against the manufacturer's manuals and FAA approved data.

04

Assembly

Rebuilt to spec with traceable parts, in a 25,000 sq ft air conditioned facility built for the tolerances this work runs to.

05

Test

Run on our own stands — universal flow and pressure, fuel control, APU fuel control, engine pump and boost pump stands. Nothing released on someone else's equipment.

06

Release

Documentation accepted across the US, Europe, the UK and China. The turn time we quote is a turn time we control.

03 — Why it matters

Time on wing
is the number.

Most shops can tell you where your unit sits in their process. That's job status. We track something else: how the component behaved once it went back on the aircraft.

When a component comes back early, the repair is the smallest part of it. There's the bench slot you had scheduled, the aircraft or engine you had promised to release, and the credibility you spend with your own customer. Time on wing is the number that matters — and it's the one we measure ourselves against.
