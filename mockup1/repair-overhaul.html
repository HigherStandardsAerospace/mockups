REPAIR VS OVERHAUL

What is the difference between a repair and an overhaul?

A repair returns a component to serviceable condition by correcting the specific discrepancy found. An overhaul returns the component to the condition and life limits defined by the manufacturer’s manual, which means full disassembly, inspection of every part against manual limits, replacement of everything outside those limits, reassembly and full acceptance test. The practical difference is what you get back. A repaired unit is airworthy. An overhauled unit is airworthy and its service life is reset against the manual.

When a repair is the right call

A repair makes sense when the discrepancy is known, contained, and isn’t a symptom of wear across the whole unit. A component that came off for one identified fault, with service history you trust and time remaining against its limits, doesn’t need taking back to zero to go back on the aircraft. A repair comes back faster, and when you’re holding an aircraft that matters. The question worth asking isn’t how the two workscopes compare on paper. It’s whether the unit is going to come back. Repairs go wrong when they’re chosen off the quote instead of off the condition of the unit. If this is the third discrepancy on the same accessory in two years, a repair isn’t solving anything. It’s booking the problem into the next removal.

When an overhaul is worth the difference

An overhaul is the right call when you need the unit back predictable, not just airworthy. Full disassembly, inspection against manual limits and replacement of everything outside them means the component comes back with its service life reset instead of carrying an unknown amount of wear. That predictability is the thing you’re buying. For an airline it’s an interval you can plan maintenance around. For an engine shop it’s one fewer variable inside a turn time you’ve already committed to somebody else. Judge an overhaul on the workscope alone and it looks like more than the job needs. Judge it on how long the unit stays on the aircraft and it usually isn’t. That’s the conversation we’d rather have.

What this means for resale value

For a broker or trader, the workscope is part of the asset. Overhauled condition with complete documentation from an approved repair station is what makes a unit moveable, because the buyer on the other side is pricing the risk that the paperwork will not clear their own inspection. A repaired unit can be perfectly serviceable and still sit, simply because it’s harder to place, and every month it sits is a month it isn’t earning.
