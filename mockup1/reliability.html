RELIABILITY AND TURN TIME

We track reliability on every part number

Our quality department maintains a tracking program that records reliability data on every part number we support. That means we can show you how a specific component has performed in service after leaving our shop, not only that it passed acceptance test on the day it shipped. A shop can usually tell you where your unit sits in its process. That’s job status. What we record is how the component behaved once it was back on the aircraft, which is the part nobody sees from the bench.

Why time on wing is the number that matters

When a component comes back early, the repair is the smallest part of it. There’s the bench slot you cleared, the aircraft or engine you’re holding, and the turn time you promised your own customer. A repair that comes back in half the interval hasn’t saved you anything. It has moved the problem to a worse moment.

Turn time

Turn time is the second question every customer asks, and most shops answer it with a shop-wide average that tells you nothing about the accessory you’re actually sending. We quote turn time by component family and by workscope, at the point of quote, against the specific unit. Because every stage from receipt through acceptance test happens in our building, that number is ours to control rather than a best guess built on a subcontractor’s schedule.

What you’re actually buying

A quote covers one repair. What decides the value of a component is how long it stays on the aircraft. That’s the figure we track and the one we’d rather talk about, because it’s the one that shows up in your schedule, your bench capacity and your commitments to your own customers.
